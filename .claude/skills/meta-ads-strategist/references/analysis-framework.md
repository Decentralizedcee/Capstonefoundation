# Ad & Competitor Analysis Framework

Use this checklist when analyzing an ad, a Branded Content post, or a landing
page. Not every field applies to every piece of material — skip what's not
present rather than padding the write-up.

## 1. Creative breakdown

- **Format**: static image, carousel, single video, Reels/Stories-native,
  Branded Content collab.
- **Hook**: what stops the scroll in the first line of copy or first 1-3
  seconds of video? Is it a question, a bold claim, a pattern interrupt, a
  relatable scenario?
- **Structure**: problem → agitation → solution? Testimonial-led? Listicle
  ("3 reasons why...")? Founder-to-camera? Before/after?
- **Visual style**: produced/polished vs. UGC/native, text-on-screen density,
  brand presence (logo, colors) vs. platform-native look.
- **Copy**: headline, primary text, description, CTA button label. Tone
  (urgent, aspirational, educational, humorous).
- **Offer**: discount, free trial, lead magnet, donation ask, event signup —
  what's actually being asked of the viewer.
- **Ad Library metadata** (when available): how long has it been running,
  which platforms/placements, how many variations of the same core idea is
  the advertiser testing.

## 2. Branded Content specifics

When the material is a creator/influencer collab rather than a brand-run ad:

- How does the creator introduce the brand — does it feel native to their
  usual content or clearly inserted?
- What's the creator's existing audience relationship being leveraged
  (expertise, relatability, aspirational lifestyle)?
- Does the messaging differ from the brand's own direct ads, and if so, how?

## 3. Landing page follow-through

Crawl the linked page with Firecrawl before commenting on it. Then check:

- Does the page deliver on the ad's specific promise/hook, or is there a
  drop-off between what was claimed and what's shown?
- Is the primary CTA on the page consistent with the ad's CTA?
- Page structure: above-the-fold clarity, social proof, objection handling,
  friction in the conversion path (form length, number of clicks to convert).
- Note any mismatch explicitly — it's often the most actionable finding.

## 4. Pattern-finding across multiple ads

When you have more than one ad from the same competitor, or ads from several
competitors:

- What hooks, offers, or visual styles repeat? Repetition over time in the Ad
  Library usually signals something is working for them.
- What messaging angle dominates (price/value, status/aspiration, fear of
  missing out, social proof, mission/values-driven)?
- Are there gaps — angles nobody in the set is using — that could be a
  differentiation opportunity?

## 5. Keyword and phrase search

When asked to search ads/social pages for brand-relevant keywords:

- Note which specific words and phrases recur across competitor ad copy,
  page headlines, and captions.
- Distinguish category-generic language (terms everyone uses) from
  distinctive phrases a specific competitor owns.
- Flag terms that map to our brand's positioning as candidates worth testing
  in our own copy, and terms that are already crowded/generic as lower
  priority.

## 6. Turning findings into recommendations

Every analysis should end with at least one of:

- A new ad/content idea, described concretely enough to brief a creative off
  of (not just "do something like this").
- A validation or challenge of an existing idea, with the specific evidence
  that supports the call.
- A concrete change to an existing ad (ours or a competitor's) — what
  exactly would change (hook, pacing, offer, CTA, visual) and the reasoning.

Avoid generic marketing platitudes ("use social proof," "create urgency")
detached from what was actually observed in the material.
