# Sparkle text: Capstone craft guide

**Status:** owner-approved style reference for **Stories (blog) and any new copy**. It sits on top of the spec's voice (hopeful, Christian, inspirational; short sentences; plain words). It never replaces it.

**Source:** an article on "sparkle text" supplied by the owner (it links to aprildavila.com and hireawriter.us). The owner said: *"This is how I would like most of the content to sound, especially the blog posts."* This file is a distilled guide, not a copy of the article.

**Where it applies**
| Content | Sparkle level |
|---|---|
| Stories (blog posts) | **Yes, moderate.** 2 to 4 sparkle moments per post (working default; the owner can change it). |
| New copy the owner approves (banners, short intros) | Light: one line at most. |
| **Fixed page copy from the spec** (Home, About, Programs, Get involved, Donate, Contact, testimonials) | **Never.** Word for word, exactly as written. |
| Scripture | **Never sparkled.** Quote exactly (NIV). |

---

## 1. What sparkle text is

Standout lines that **glitter with creativity and emotional truth**. They make the reader pause, nod or feel something, and stay with them afterwards. A fresh metaphor, a quiet turn of phrase, a line that lands.

**Four marks of sparkle**
1. **Evocative imagery.** A picture the reader can see.
2. **Emotional resonance.** A feeling or a universal truth (love, loss, wonder, hope).
3. **Rhythmic flow.** A musical, lyrical quality (cadence, a little alliteration or assonance).
4. **Originality.** A fresh angle or a gentle surprise.

## 2. How to write it

1. **Use metaphors and similes, but not clichés.** Look at the thing again. Do not reach for "beacon of hope", "tapestry", "journey" or "shining light".
2. **Play with rhythm and sound.** Read it aloud. Short, then a little longer. Let the cadence carry the feeling.
3. **Touch strong, universal emotion with fresh words.** Hope, loss, comfort, belonging, courage.
4. **Be specific.** Concrete details beat general statements. ("The kettle on the stove" beats "a warm home".) Specific detail must be true or clearly general imagery. See guardrail 3.
5. **Balance.** Not every sentence shimmers. Too much sparkle overwhelms the reader. Let plain, clear sentences carry the information and let a few lines shine.

## 3. Where to place it in a Story

1. **The opening line.** The hook. Pair it with the human moment or promise (see `humor.md`).
2. **A turning point.** Where the post moves from the problem to the way forward.
3. **A key idea.** The one sentence a reader would underline.
4. **The closing line.** An invitation, a prayer, or a quiet challenge.

## 4. Capstone guardrails (these override the general technique)

1. **Plain words, short sentences stay.** Sparkle lives inside a short, plain sentence. No purple paragraphs.
2. **Imagery palette: hopeful and grounded.** Foundations, cornerstones, homes, doors, tables, bread, lamps, dawn, seeds and rain, roots, hands, roads, shelter. **Avoid** war, dragons, gods, battlefields, blood, horror, or anything dramatic for its own sake.
3. **Honesty first.** Sparkle is imagery, **never invented facts, people, events or results.** A metaphor must not smuggle in a claim ("Capstone has rescued thousands"). Facts come only from `stats.md`; stories about people only from `stories.md` with consent.
4. **Dignity.** Never make a real person's suffering decorative. No poverty porn, no guilt, no sensational pain. Spend sparkle on **hope, comfort and action**, not on describing the hurt.
5. **No humor.** The source article also teaches playful, witty and sarcastic sparkle. **That part is off** here (see `humor.md`). Warm, gentle lightness is fine.
6. **Scripture is never sparkled.** Quote it exactly in the NIV (see `CLAUDE.md` section 7). Never paraphrase it as if quoting.
7. **Fixed spec copy is never touched.**
8. **Do not reuse the sample lines below.** They show the technique. Write fresh ones for each post.
9. **Keep the brand's location clear** where it matters (Nigeria, Enugu, Abuja) without turning it into a metaphor.

## 5. Plain → sparkle (style samples, not facts, not to be reused)

| Plain | Sparkle |
|---|---|
| Hope is important when life is hard. | Hope rarely arrives with a trumpet. It comes like bread on a table: ordinary, and exactly enough. |
| Grief is a long process. | Grief is a long road, and nobody should walk it alone. |
| Skills help people stand on their own. | Skills are a quiet kind of rescue: they stay with you when the helpers go home. |
| A church can care for widows. | A church is a house with many doors. Who has not yet been invited in? |
| Prayer matters. | Prayer opens the door. A steady hand walks you through it. |

**From the owner's reference article (for study only, never reuse):** "Grief is the weight of a thousand unsaid goodbyes." · "Love is not the absence of fear, but the courage to walk into it holding someone's hand." · "Time didn't heal all wounds; it simply taught the scars how to dance."

## 6. Quick check before a post is finished

- [ ] 2 to 4 sparkle moments, not more. Opening, turning point, closing at least.
- [ ] Each one is a short, plain sentence. Each is original, not a cliché.
- [ ] No invented fact, person, event or result hiding inside an image.
- [ ] Nothing makes a real person's pain decorative.
- [ ] No jokes, sarcasm or wordplay at anyone's expense.
- [ ] Scripture is exact NIV, cited, and not embellished.
- [ ] Read aloud: it sounds warm, steady and hopeful.
